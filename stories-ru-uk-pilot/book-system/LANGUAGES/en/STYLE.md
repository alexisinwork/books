# Literary English

Write idiomatic contemporary English with the narrator's and characters' individual registers intact. The author selected `en-US` on 14 September 2026 for Riokka, intended for readers in the US, UK and Australia. This is a spelling/punctuation convention, not a promise of identical tastes across markets. Another book may explicitly select `en-GB`. Never mix house orthography by chapter.

Preserve viewpoint, tense, uncertainty, emotional restraint, comic timing and the reader's access to information. Ukrainian or Russian syntax is not binding. Reshape sentences and dialogue naturally while preserving every meaningful beat, implication, ambiguity and payoff. Distinguish natural rephrasing from adding an explanation.

Keep the fictional setting and its cultural identity. Do not relocate characters, convert backgrounds to American/British stereotypes, invent dialects, replace names with English names, or substitute unrelated cultural jokes. Register and social relationship matter more than decorative regional slang. Jeeves-like formality, where actually established, can coexist with US house spelling.

Maintain a glossary of names, shortened forms, ship names, titles and technical terms. Separate a plot-critical recurring image from ordinary idiom. Record exceptions when a shared English synonym would erase a contrast. Preserve numbers, units, manifests, document voices and typographic emphasis; conversions require an explicit policy and semantic checking.

Review articles, collocations, prepositions, pronoun reference, tense/aspect, dialogue punctuation, accidental formality, repetitive sentence patterns and calques. Run a target-only read without source text or corrective hints. Human fiction line editing addresses sentence craft; copyediting and beta reading have different scopes. A native speaker is not automatically a trained editor, and model approval is not evidence of human reception.

For a new English original, apply the same prose checks but do not invent a Ukrainian source or run fidelity comparison against a nonexistent original.
