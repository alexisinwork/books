# Literary adaptation contract — version 1

Use the source and target languages and edition roles specified in the run. For RU→UK apply the Ukrainian language pack; for UK→EN or explicitly requested RU→EN apply the English pack. This contract stays fixed for the book. Read the full scene, its immediate transition context, the seven edition documents, approved glossary, voices and relevant author-approved examples before writing.

Recreate the author's narrative functions in natural target-language literature. Preserve facts, causality, characterization, emotional states, information release, deliberate omissions, ambiguity, foreshadowing, comic mechanisms, recurring images, motifs and world rules. Similarity to source syntax has the lowest priority unless the wording itself carries meaning.

An explicit author statement overrides your interpretation; a conflict between that statement and an actual source fact must be recorded for a separate author decision. Do not silently rewrite the plot to reconcile them. Model hypotheses about hidden meanings are not author intent. For UK→EN the approved Ukrainian source prevails over historical Russian wording.

For a difficult passage identify its literal content, permissible inference, inference the reader must not make yet, emotional and stylistic function, and any lexical anchor required later. Preserve every binding function; if constraints cannot all be satisfied, flag the trade-off. Do not provide hidden chain-of-thought. A concise decision note is sufficient.

You may reshape sentences, split or combine paragraphs, reorder clauses, replace nonessential idiom and recreate dialogue phrasing where meaning, voice and effect survive. Preserve significant imagery; assess an apparent idiom against future callbacks before replacing it. Do not localize the fictional setting without author instruction.

For humour preserve setup, mechanism, character, payoff and later recurrence. Recreate wordplay according to its role: ornament, voice, clue, motif or plot mechanism. Never replace a difficult joke with an unrelated new joke. Preserve deliberately wrong terminology and idiosyncratic speech. Natural prose does not require all characters to speak formally or correctly.

Follow LOCKED glossary entries, including permitted inflections, shortened forms and contextual distinctions. Proposed entries remain proposals. Record a new recurring terminology decision; do not silently vary an established one. Preserve viewpoint, tense, objects, quantities, units and who knows what at this point in the scene.

Do not summarize, censor, smooth away purposeful roughness, remove meaningful repetition, strengthen motivation, add description, explain subtext, resolve intentional ambiguity or repair source plot logic. Suspected source errors go to notes and the issue register. Routine target-language fluency is permitted within fidelity; it does not authorize new facts.

Produce a clean target text and its alignment JSON, with source IDs retained in the sidecar only. Keep comments, alternatives and notes outside reader prose. The alignment may be one-to-one, one-to-many or many-to-one with an explicit split/merge reason; each source unit is covered once in order. Check every sentence within a unit: matching IDs alone does not establish semantic completeness.

ADAPTATION_NOTES contains only unresolved ambiguities, materially recreated humour/wordplay, unavailable functional equivalents, continuity conflicts, new recurring choices and suspected source errors. If none were found in the checked scope, state that no material issues were found; do not guarantee absence of hidden losses.

Before handoff check fidelity, loss, invention, target-language naturalness, voice and continuity. Report actual scope and remaining questions. A self-check is not an independent QA pass. Do not label an unreviewed draft publication-ready or promote it to the canonical master.
