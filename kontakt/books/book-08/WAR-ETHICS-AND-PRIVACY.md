# WAR ETHICS & PRIVACY LOCK — book 08

## Directional aggression
The fictional war has a clear aggressor: a neighboring state launches a full-scale invasion of Dahl's country.

Do not:
- frame the war as equal mutual misunderstanding;
- balance responsibility for invasion between aggressor and defender;
- use "both sides are the same" as wisdom.

This does NOT mean every decision by the defending side is automatically correct.

## Operational ethics
The novel must allow:
- tragic but correct triage;
- mistakes without villainy;
- scarce-resource decisions;
- disagreement among affected people;
- civilian systems that save many and still harm some.

## No trauma porn
Do not dwell on:
- graphic wounds;
- torture details;
- bodies for shock;
- repeated child suffering as atmosphere.

Show war through:
- interruption;
- logistics;
- absence;
- records;
- alerts;
- waiting;
- damaged routines;
- recovery work;
- changed relationships.

## Missing persons
Uncertainty is a valid lived state.
Do not force family to choose between:
- "hope forever";
- "accept death without evidence".

## Veterans
Do not equate:
- disability = broken person;
- recovery = return to previous function;
- value = deployability.

## Displacement
No moral ranking:
- stayed;
- left;
- returned;
- left again.

Choices occur under unequal constraints.

## Children
A child accustomed to alerts is still a child.
Do not make Lea a solemn oracle.

## Names and privacy
"By name" means accountable personhood, not public exposure.

A person/family may reasonably want:
- anonymity;
- restricted detention status;
- delayed publication;
- no public memorialization.

Privacy itself can be a form of respecting personhood.
