## Decisions that should be reconciled

The exact source quotes, hashes, dependencies and proposed responses are in [decision-ledger.json](decision-ledger.json). Each item remains **unresolved / proposed**; matching entries are recorded in the affected books' audit registers.

### 1. Book 1: use the information that already exists

The new plan says Nina's canon was unavailable. The repository defines her as Dal's former partner, dead after a long illness; he did not cause her death, but withdrew emotionally into self-companion support. The technical template is **Dal's safe-presence design from that period**, not evidence that Nina invented it or survives inside software.

The repository also defines deep accompaniment, direct windows and an approximately eleven-day timeline. These are available references, not questions that require inventing another answer.

The new plan moves the practical establishment of R1 toward the end and changes Maya's payoff from independent neutral accommodation to a boundary-setting conversation with Gor. Both could be worked into an edition, but they require a recorded mapping. My preference: retain the early breakthrough and later tested consolidation of R1; retain Maya's independent departure as the protected final consequence. A conversation with Gor can support that departure if adopted.

### 2. Book 2: choose whether the betrayal returns

The repository explicitly removes the secret traitor and makes failure arise from public visibility, infrastructure resilience and the movement's incomplete theory. The new plan introduces Relay's leak as one of five causes. It is not a sole-cause betrayal, but it is still a substantive reversal.

**My preference: retain the no-traitor architecture.** It makes this book less repetitive after Gor and leaves the advance-capacity evidence stronger as a mystery leading to Predictariat. Keep the new attention mechanics and Lana material where compatible. Keep Taras an independent continuity worker; examine whether restoring his civil identity recreates the rescue structure the plan says it avoids.

Specify that emergency and identity infrastructure survive the ranking outage. The new plan's references to emergency messages competing in raw feeds need a clearly bounded meaning.

### 3. Books 3–5: preserve a traceable chain of people and evidence

Choose Osya's profession once and propagate it through Books 4–8. My preference is to retain the courier work already connected to the series' manual networks; the predicted-choice payoff does not require changing professions.

Book 3 introduces a deadline of less than sixty hours on Day 1, but resolves a later deadline on Day 5. If these are separate reviews, the first needs an outcome and the second needs an explicit extension/new offer. Otherwise revise the clock.

Book 4 ends with **K-17 as a recipient** of affect services. Book 5's new Lev is a **worker** in Supported Contribution. Do not silently equate them. Keep K-17's client-side trail and introduce Lev explicitly as the worker that trail reveals, or record a deliberate redesign of Book 4.

### 4. Book 5: decide the entire causal package around Taras

The repository uses emergency split-relay followed by a delayed fatal complication. The new plan uses manual equipment isolation followed by a fatal hardware failure. It also replaces Dal actually accepting one transparent relief session with repeatedly stopping before it starts.

These are different stories. My editorial preference is the repository's delayed loss and genuinely helpful relief session: they resist a familiar “operator dies holding the switch” climax and give relief more than a verbal defense. This is an editorial recommendation, not a scientific endorsement of the fictional mechanism.

If choosing the new accident instead, explain why human-session demand overloads power/cooling, why manual routing defeats physical protection, and why a safe remote shutdown cannot work. Seeding “stop-path” alone does not complete that causal chain.

Restore a concrete Book 5 ending clue about **child cohorts / upstream Guidance**. The new emergency-anesthesia ending points toward Book 7, while Book 6 begins with a fresh invitation. Theme connects them, but the existing investigative chain is weakened.

### 5. Book 6: settle family identity and POV access

Marta as Mila's mother creates a strong independent parental conflict. It is a viable proposed change, not a detail the earlier architecture already established. Decide how her deceased older son fits the family, or explicitly supersede that backstory.

Keep Mila's requested return to support. It is one of the best additions: consent remains real when she chooses comfort.

In Chapter 16, specify why Dal can hear Marta and Zhdan's intimate conversation. Apply the same check to other apparently autonomous scenes across the plans. A plan stating first-person POV does not by itself supply access to every scene.

### 6. Book 7: protect the personal ending

The repository deliberately makes the workshop viable without Dal's signature. His five-year commitment is for his own future. The new plot again makes a major act necessary to preserve Mila's and Osya's arrangements, then places the personal home choice in the coda.

**My strongest structural recommendation: keep the workshop decision as the climax.** The new statement/review material can be supporting action if desired, but should not replace the moment when Dal chooses a future without a client or dependent providing the reason. This also preserves Book 8's workshop-turned-aid-hub payoff.

The return to full smoothing/default stability after refusal also needs reconciliation with the rights achieved in Books 3 and 6. Explicitly state what expires, what remains protected, and what appeals can preserve.

### 7. Book 8: select a complete edition, then verify its logistics

Repository: Ilya/Nadya/Leya/Sofia Varan, Eva Rud, civilian disappearance and later verified return from detention, nine months into war, about six weeks of action.

Downloads: Yan/Dana Kosta, Lina Ors, Nika Veres, unidentified hospital patient M-17, separate displaced family, Mila as the child line, seven weeks into war, nine days of action.

My preference is to retain the connected Varan family and longer timescale, while considering the new repeated triage test and clear three-part interface distinction. The choice is not a name replacement: it changes relationships, the kind of loss and the process of return.

If adopting the M-17 version, make hospital locations and transfer purposes explicit: he is already said to be in the safer Western belt, yet later scenes treat transport as part of the rescue bottleneck. What care requires this transfer, from where to where, and what makes waiting acceptable?

Day 9 currently contains Chapters 28–36: reform, multiple human resolutions and further crisis work. Keep any immediate change a bounded pilot, with authority, staffing and follow-up; allow later time for broader institutional and recovery consequences.

## Series-level risks worth addressing

- **Repetition:** each book can become “useful institution → Dal tries to fix a person → method fails → consent-based reform.” Preserve different engines: intimate heist, ensemble operation, detective story, institutional contest, loss, family drama, personal commitment, wartime triage.
- **Premature completion of Ballast's arc:** Books 1, 5 and 7 each describe a seemingly final recognition that he is a tool. Distinguish an early stated rule from the later costly ability to live by it.
- **Overly uniform chapter units:** the new plans have usable maps, but many chapters have exactly three teaching beats. Merge or vary units by dramatic movement. Neither three scenes nor an irreversible decision in every quiet scene is a quality requirement.
- **Knowledge access:** document inserts can put the reader ahead; Dal still needs a later evidentiary route. Check offstage betrayal, family talks, medical findings and institutional authority.
- **Unproven readiness boxes:** chapter plans' checked boxes are authoring claims, not independent verification. Missing biographies, clocks and cross-book changes remain open despite the labels.
